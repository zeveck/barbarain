# BarbarAIn — creative brief

**In one sentence:** a modern, tap-or-click text adventure RPG in the Hyborian
Age, where a laconic Cimmerian thief and his companions explore, fight, solve
Monkey-Island-style puzzles and uncover a conspiracy that turns out to be far
stranger (and far more "AI") than sword-and-sorcery usually allows.

**The feeling:** pulpy, sweaty, dangerous sword-and-sorcery with Howard's
gloomy grandeur and sudden gigantic mirth, plus the "wait, WHAT?" pleasure of
a story that keeps pulling the rug, and the cozy click of a good RPG loop
(loot, levels, gear).

## What they asked for

- [ ] "modern-leaning text adventure" that "describes scenes"
- [ ] explore dungeons
- [ ] fight monsters
- [ ] gain levels, HP, MP and magic
- [ ] "a range of weapons"
- [ ] "typical RPG elements"
- [ ] "relatively simple 16-bit style graphics" of environments, towns people,
      the hero's party and enemies
- [ ] playable on desktop and mobile
- [ ] called **BarbarAIn**
- [ ] heavily based on Conan's world as written by Robert E. Howard
- [ ] characters evoking those from the books and movies, behaving "in character"
- [ ] a new story
- [ ] at least three major twists/surprises that make sense in the world
- [ ] loose ideas offered ("anything goes"): robots from the future, time
      travel, pirates, trans-dimensional dragons, a heist
- [ ] light adventure-game puzzles "a la Secret of Monkey Island"
- [ ] items, armor and weapons
- [ ] a good quality-of-life inventory system
- [ ] quality over speed: "racing to say done but producing slop is a fail"

## Who, where, how long

Adults and teens who like RPGs, Howard's stories or point-and-click adventures.
Desktop (mouse and keyboard, optional typed commands) and phones (portrait,
one thumb). Total play about 2 to 2.5 hours across five chapters; a session is
one chapter or a dungeon (15 to 30 minutes), with saving anywhere outside
battle so a bus ride is enough.

## Style references

- **Robert E. Howard's Conan stories** (Hyborian Age): Shadizar "the
  Wicked", Zamora, Stygia and Khemi, the Black Coast and Barachan pirates,
  Hyrkanian nomads, Crom, Set, Mitra. Borrow: the geography and gods (from
  Howard's own world-building), the tone (vivid, sensual, fatalistic prose; a
  barbarian contemptuous of civilization and sorcery), the recurring motifs
  (thieves' towers, serpent cults, lost cities with strange alien or
  super-scientific relics such as the star-being in a sorcerer's tower).
  Avoid: quoting Howard's or the films' text, and using his characters' or
  the films' characters' names. Characters are **original but evoke** them:
  - Kord of Cimmeria evokes Conan (the player character)
  - Toktai the Hyrkanian archer-thief evokes Subotai (film)
  - Red Valka, pirate captain, evokes Bêlit and Valeria
  - Thulamon, archpriest of Set, evokes Thoth-Amon and Thulsa Doom
  - Ashkarran the Veiled, tower sorcerer, evokes Yara of the Elephant Tower
  - Bakari of Kush evokes the Kushite warrior companions of the pastiches/films
  - Ain, the narrator and "wizard", evokes the film's wizard-chronicler
  The name "Conan" itself is a trademark, so the hero is an original
  Cimmerian who plays like him.
- **16-bit JRPGs** (SNES era): side-view turn-based battles with the party on
  the right and monsters on the left over a location backdrop; small
  character sprites; 64px dialogue portraits. Borrow the look and battle
  layout, not any game's characters or art.
- **The Secret of Monkey Island** (puzzles): borrow the inventory-and-dialogue
  puzzle logic (use X on Y, give, combine), comic timing and the "you can't die
  from a puzzle" kindness. Avoid its jokes, characters, insult sword-fighting
  and any text.
- **Modern text adventures** (Inkle-style choice games): no typing required,
  clickable choices and keywords; an optional command line for those who like
  to type.

## Decisions made on the person's behalf

- Platform both, web target, budget $20 (defaults). Scope treated as **large**
  because the request describes a full RPG with five-act story; the cut list
  protects the finish.
- Original characters that evoke Howard's and the films' (see above), no
  quotations; the Hyborian geography and gods are used.
- Of the loose ideas, the story uses **all five**: a heist (twice), pirates,
  a trans-dimensional "dragon" (a bronze ship that swims through time),
  robots from the future (bronze automata) and time travel, all justified by
  one idea that fits the title: a thinking machine (an AI) from 12,000 years
  in the future. Howard himself put a star-being in a sorcerer's tower and
  radium-lit lost cities in his stories, so it belongs.
- Phone layout is portrait (text reads best tall); landscape also works.
