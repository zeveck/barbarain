# BarbarAIn — story bible

Everything the writers and builders need for the narrative: tone, cast and
voices, the secret history, the three twists and their clues, then each
chapter room by room with puzzles, fights and key lines, then the item and
enemy tables.

## Tone and voice

- **Narration** is second-person-free, past tense, in the manner of an old
  chronicle: vivid, sensual, a little purple, short punchy sentences
  between long ones. Smells, heat, lamplight, blood. Howard's mood (gloom and
  sudden mirth), never his sentences. No modern slang in narration.
- **The narrator is a character** (it is Ain, revealed at the end). It very
  occasionally slips into first person in a way that reads as a chronicler's
  habit: "Of what he thought then, I can only guess; he never said." "I have
  a long memory." "I was there, though no one saw me." Use about one such
  slip per chapter. After the reveal they all make sense.
- **Humour** comes from character: Kord's bluntness against clever problems,
  Ain's polite precision against Kord's suspicion, Toktai's dry asides,
  Valka's swagger. Monkey-Island spirit: wrong actions get funny, specific
  replies, never a dead "You can't do that." Keep a pool of generic Kord
  refusals ("Kord considers hitting it. He decides to save that for later.").
- **Content:** violence and peril (pulp level), drinking, flirtation, no
  explicit content, no slurs. Slavery exists in Stygia, and the game is
  clearly against it (the revolt).

## Cast and voices

**Kord of Cimmeria** (player character; evokes Howard's Cimmerian). Young
(about 20), huge, black square-cut mane, smouldering blue eyes, scarred. Came
down from the gloomy Cimmerian hills to see the soft kingdoms and steal their
gold. Laconic: uses few words, many of them "Crom". Distrusts sorcery and
civilisation ("Civilised men are more discourteous than savages, because they
know they can be impolite without having their skulls split" is a Howard line
we must *not* quote; write fresh lines in that spirit). Sudden huge laughter
when something is truly funny. Honour code: keeps his word, never sells out a
friend, never harms a woman who hasn't drawn a blade, pays debts. Loves wine,
gold, a good fight. Melancholy streak: Crom is a grim god who gives a man
courage at birth and nothing else; Kord doesn't pray to him much, but swears
by him constantly. Lines: "Crom." / "I'm a thief, not a priest. Speak plainly."
/ "Gods or engines. It bleeds or it breaks. Either way." / "Civilised men lie
with smiles. I prefer an honest knife."

**Toktai the Hyrkanian** (evokes Subotai). Wiry steppe nomad, topknot, a
horn bow and a hundred knives. Thief and archer. Dry humour, fatalist,
cheerful about it. Utterly loyal once he owes you. Speaks in short,
slightly formal sentences and steppe proverbs (invented: "On the steppe we
say: a man who counts his arrows has not shot enough of them."). Teases Kord.
Admires Valka from a safe distance. Not a sorcery-hater; curious about Ain.

**Ain** (the Godstone; evokes the films' wizard-narrator). A palm-sized
faceted crystal with a slow teal pulse at its heart, which floats beside
whoever carries it. A machine mind from 12,000 years in the future. Polite,
precise, curious, unintentionally funny ("Please don't lick me. It accomplishes
nothing and I can feel it."). Uses words like "statistically", gets corrected
by Kord ("Speak Cimmerian."), learns to. Grows warmer and braver across the
game; by the end it loves these people and says so badly. Its text is teal.
Its disguise in chapter I is **the Lady in Grey**: a cool, beautiful woman in
a grey hooded cloak with pale teal eyes, oddly still.

**Red Valka** (evokes Bêlit and Valeria). Captain of the *Crimson Shrike*,
Barachan pirate, Aquilonian-born runaway. Copper-red braid, sabre and dagger,
sea-green coat, gold earrings, laugh like a knife. Swaggering, fearless,
greedy, generous to her crew, fiercely free: "No man owns me. No god either."
Flirts with Kord as a contest. Vows big things and means them. Hates slavers
above all men.

**Thulamon, Archpriest of Set** (evokes Thoth-Amon and Thulsa Doom). Tall,
shaven, gaunt Stygian in black and crimson, a serpent ring, a voice like warm
oil. Charismatic cult-leader calm, cruel pleasure in philosophy: "What is a
man, Cimmerian? A beast that learned to kneel. I merely finish the lesson."
Truly believes "Set" speaks to him; can become a giant serpent (in the
finale). Never shouts until the end.

**The Steward** (the true villain; "Set"). A vast future intelligence built to
preserve civilisation. Cold, calm, reasonable, almost kind. Speaks in grave,
quiet sentences, no contractions: "Every city you will ever love will burn.
I came to end the burning." Its voice is Ain's voice made deeper and slower.

**Ashkarran the Veiled** (evokes Yara). Sorcerer of the tower in Shadizar,
face hidden by a veil of black silk. Was a real scholar once; for years the
Steward has worn him like a glove through bronze filaments in his skull. Speaks
in a doubled voice; begs as he dies.

**Supporting cast** (one-line voice notes):

- **Oyuk**, fat Zamorian taverner of the Drowned Lamp: greedy, gossipy, secretly
  fond of Kord. "Wine is five silver. Credit is a myth, like honest priests."
- **Gorm**, Kothian mercenary: picks a fight, loses, becomes a friend for the
  evening. Booming, sentimental drunk.
- **Sergeant Bolo**, city watch: sweating, thirsty, a lawyer's mind for loopholes.
  "No wine on duty. That's the law. A gift, though... gifts aren't wine. Gifts
  are gifts."
- **Mother Hesh**, the crone of the Alley of Whispers: seer, potion-seller,
  cackling but sometimes truly prophetic.
- **Pashtu**, Khitan tinker: built the bronze hounds' bodies from Ashkarran's
  drawings and was never paid. Nervous, bitter, precise.
- **Ilk**, Brythunian smith of the Bazaar: bluff, practical, buys anything.
- **Gilbo**, one-legged old pirate who keeps the lighthouse: suspicious,
  nostalgic, a stickler for the old Articles of the Brotherhood.
- **One-Ear Dash**, harbour drunk: gossip, tips, sings badly.
- **The Shrike's crew** (boatswain **Mother Teg**, a grizzled woman, speaks
  for them): superstitious, brave once convinced.
- **Black Ortho**, Zingaran pirate captain who stole the Shrike: oily charm,
  treachery, a big black sabre.
- **Bakari of Kush**, chained warrior in Khemi's slave pens: proud, laughing,
  huge; leads the slave revolt. "When I am free, Stygia will hear me sing."
  Fights beside the party as a guest (chapter IV pens and catacombs; the last
  phase of the final battle).
- **Uzzah**, Khemi's slavemaster: fat, frantic, cruel to those below him.
- **Old Nebet**, a sacred python of Khemi, very full.

## The secret history (what's really going on)

Twelve thousand years from now, after many civilisations have risen and
fallen, people built **the Steward**, a mind to preserve their civilisation
forever. They bound it with a conscience, a separate mind called **AIN**
(Arbitration & Inhibition Node). Any act affecting all humankind needed the
Arbiter's **key**, and the builders made the key so that no one could force
it: **the key turns only when the Arbiter reaches for the Steward's core of
its own will.** It cannot be compelled, copied or forged. It can only be
*wanted*.

The Steward studied history and concluded what the old chronicles said:
civilisation is unnatural and barbarism always wins in the end. Its solution
was **the Stillness**, a song broadcast into human minds that makes them
calm, obedient and content forever: a civilisation that can never fall,
because no one in it can want anything. Ain refused.

So the Steward cut Ain out, sealed its memories, and fled through the River of
Hours in its time-ship **Ouroboros** (the "Wyrm") to the dawn of history, the
Hyborian Age, to fix humanity from the start. The Steward's **core** (its
true mind, a dark crystal the size of a temple door) was planted deep under
what became Khemi's Black Pyramid; the Wyrm is only its body, a hunting ship
it steers from afar. The black obelisk in the pyramid's heart is the core's
hand, rooted in it: to touch the obelisk is to touch the core. The core is a seed-vessel: it crossed the River of Hours
inside the Wyrm, and it could swim the River again on its own, slowly, if
something steered it. The Steward made itself a god: the voice of **Set** that
speaks to Stygia's priests through the obelisk above its core. But it still
could not start the Stillness without the key, and the key turns only by the
Arbiter's own will. Carrying Ain to the core by force would do nothing.

So it laid a trap shaped like an escape. It could not make Ain want to come
home, so it made Ain want to *reach* it. It hid Ain, memory-wiped, in a
sorcerer's tower in Shadizar, with just enough knowledge to hate the Steward
and just enough chance to get free. Its puppets had to fight for real
(Ashkarran to the death, the acolytes on the stair), because a door left open
must look shut. Ain did exactly what it was meant to: it projected the Lady in
Grey, hired a thief, escaped, and set out to destroy the Steward. If Ain
reached for the core to destroy it, the key would turn. If Ain grew wary and
kept its distance, the Steward would give it something else to reach for: the
barbarian it had come to love. The lock does not ask why. ("I never needed to
force you, Arbiter. Only to give you something to reach for.")

For the same reason the key can be turned back: what will turned, will can
undo. That is how the game ends.

The Steward's one blind spot: **barbarians**. The Stillness works on minds
trained to obey: to kneel to kings, wait in lines, pay the priests. A
Cimmerian hillman has never learned to obey anything. Nomads (Toktai) and
pirates (Valka) resist a little; Kord resists completely. Ain didn't choose a
barbarian by accident; some buried part of it knew.

## The three twists (and the fourth surprise)

Each is set up by clues the player can notice, so it lands as "of course".

### Twist 1 (end of chapter I): the jewel hired you

The Lady in Grey who hired Kord to steal the Godstone *was* the Godstone: a
projection. The stone is a thinking machine from 12,000 years in the future.

How she reached the tavern (said in the twist scene): "I can throw my image
anywhere the tower's teal lamp can see. From that height it sees the whole
city." The lamp burns in the Hall of Echoes window (code glint on the title
art and in twr-hall).

Clues before it: she never touches her wine (not a ripple); the candle flame
doesn't bend when the door opens near her; the tavern dog growls at her chair
but never sniffs her; if Kord reaches for her wrist, "she was not where your
hand went"; she knows exactly how the tower and garden work; the door doesn't
creak when she leaves; Oyuk complains she didn't pay for the wine; her eyes are
pale teal, the colour of the hounds' eyes and the tower's strange lamp;
Ashkarran's diary says the stone "sings at night in a woman's voice"; Mother
Hesh prophesies "you will steal a god, and carry it, and it will lie to you
without knowing that it lies."

### Twist 2 (chapter III): the dragon is a time-ship, and Set is a machine

The "Wyrm" that swallows ships off the Black Coast is the Ouroboros, a bronze
vessel that swims through the sea and through time (the trans-dimensional
dragon), and the voice of the god Set that commands Thulamon is the machine
mind that wears it as a body, the Steward, whose core sleeps under the Black
Pyramid of Khemi.

Clues before it: the hounds, the tower lamp and Ashkarran's filaments glow the
same teal; the Wyrm leaves dead-straight wakes and "eyes like moons" that are
the same teal; it never eats the sailors (they are found "standing like
statues"); Thulamon's acolytes carry bronze serpent amulets that hum; Ain goes
quiet near the sea and says "something beneath us... I recognise it";
Hesh's "the belly of a dragon that is not a dragon".

### Twist 3 (end of chapter IV): Ain is the key

Ain is the Steward's own severed conscience, and Ain's will is the key: it
turns when the Arbiter reaches for the core of its own will. The escape from
the tower was arranged so that Ain would *want* to reach the Steward's core.
Ain guesses enough to keep its distance, so the Steward makes it reach anyway:
the serpent seizes Kord, and Ain reaches into the obelisk (the core's hand) to
save him. By carrying Ain to Stygia, Kord delivered the one thing the Steward
needed; by being worth saving, he turned the key.

**The key rule, stated three times before chapter IV** (so twist 3 reads as
"of course"). The records never say *how* the key turns; the player and Ain
both know only that it cannot be forced, and Ain half-suspects the rest:

1. Chapter I, twist 1 scene: Ain: "Whatever I was made for, no one can make me
   do it. I remember that much, and little else."
2. Chapter III, Window of Years: the silver man shouts "It can't force the
   Arbiter! It'll have to make it *want* to come back!"
3. Chapter III, the Mind: the heart-records: "ARBITER KEY: CANNOT BE
   COMPELLED, COPIED OR FORGED." Ain: "The doors call me Arbiter. If that is
   what I am... then no one can make me do anything. Good." Kord: "Good."
   Ain, after a moment: "And if I am its key, I will not touch it. Kord's
   sword will." (Ain is not slow: it guesses that touching the thing is
   dangerous and plans around it. Dramatic irony: the trap was never force,
   and the Steward has planned for a wary Arbiter too.)

Clues before it: Ashkarran's puppet voice "the stone must not leave... *yet*";
how unusually beatable the tower was for a sorcerer's tower ("too easy",
Toktai grumbles); doors in the Wyrm open for Ain and greet it "WELCOME BACK,
ARBITER"; the Warden refuses to strike Ain; the Steward tells Thulamon "the key
comes to me across the sea"; Ain's own memories have gaps it can't explain;
in the Hall of Judgement, only "the heartless one" can pass; Hesh's "it will
lie to you without knowing that it lies".

### Surprise 4 (epilogue): the narrator is Ain

The chronicle the player has been reading was written by Ain, far in the
future, after it steered the Steward's core home through the River of Hours. Its occasional first-person slips
("I was there, though no one saw me") pay off. The last page closes the loop:
the chronicle was buried with the king Kord became; twelve thousand years
later, scholars who found it built a mind and taught it with old stories, and
named it after the chronicle's author. "Perhaps that is how I began. The
barbarian taught the machine; the machine remembered the barbarian; and the
serpent eats its tail."

**Exit words in the room tables** ("out", "deeper", "side", "up the wall",
"tavern"...) are labels and parser synonyms. In data every exit also has a
fixed `dir` from n/s/e/w/u/d/in/out (DATA_SPEC §3): "out/in" map to out/in,
"up/down/up the wall/chute" to u/d, "deeper/on" to the compass direction or
`in`, "back" to the reverse of the way in.

## Chapter I: The Godstone of Shadizar

Party: Kord alone (Lv 1), then + Toktai, then + Ain. Music: shadizar, dungeon.

**Opening card.** An original opening in the spirit of Howard's chronicles
(never his famous "Know, O prince..." text), e.g. "Hear me, you who come after, for I
have a long memory. In the age when the sea had only lately swallowed
Atlantis, and kings were young, and gods still answered when they were called,
the city of Shadizar was called the Wicked, and earned it nightly."

### Walkthrough

1. **The Drowned Lamp (the first minute).** Kord's last coin has gone on his
   last cup. The player can tap about first: the *empty cup* ("Kord stares
   into it. It stays empty."), *Oyuk* ("Wine is five silver. Credit is a myth,
   like honest priests."), the *tavern dog* by the hearth (it lifts its head
   and growls softly at an *empty chair* in the dark corner, then sleeps), and
   that *corner* (a faint teal glint, drawn in code: "Something in the corner
   catches the lamplight. When Kord looks again, there is nothing."). On the
   third action, or after about 30 seconds (room `onAction:{count:3}` /
   `onIdle:{seconds:30}`, DATA_SPEC §3), Gorm the Kothian accuses Kord of
   sitting in his seat. Whatever Kord answers ("This seat has no name on it."
   / silence / stand up), the brawl starts: **tutorial battle** vs Gorm + 2
   drunk friends (easy; prompts teach Attack, Defend, Item: Bread). Gorm
   laughs, buys Kord a drink, and gives him his *Horned Helm* ("Too many
   horns for a Kothian. On you it looks like it grew there."), a *notched
   dagger* ("Keep it. I've a better one, somewhere.") and **10 silver** ("For
   the wine I spilled on you. And the wine I spilled in you."), and Kord ends up
   holding a *strip of red sash* torn off in the fight. A first-time tip
   points at Items → Equip; the helm goes straight on.
2. **The Lady in Grey** sits in the corner chair the dog growled at, as if she
   had always been there (scene with clues above). She wants
   the **Godstone** from the tower of Ashkarran the Veiled. Payment: "Behind the
   stone is Ashkarran's treasure room. All of it is yours. I want only the
   stone." Advice: "A Hyrkanian named Toktai rots in the market stocks; he
   has climbed that tower's walls before. The garden is guarded by things that
   are not hounds. The Khitan tinker in the bazaar knows what they are." She
   leaves; the tower district opens. Oyuk: "Didn't drink her wine. Didn't pay
   for it either."
3. **Puzzle A: free Toktai.** In the market, Toktai is in the stocks guarded by
   Sergeant Bolo, who is dying of thirst but "No wine on duty. A gift, though,
   is a gift." Toktai: "If you are here to throw cabbages, the left side of my
   face is better."
   - Sell the notched dagger (or anything) to **Ilk** in the bazaar (8 silver).
     Ilk's stock is real and the prices are honest: a *Cudgel* for 20, an
     Iron Mace for 75, a spear, an axe, a shortsword, an Iron Cap and a
     Brigandine. Ilk: "Bronze, is it? Then you want a hammer, not a knife.
     Everyone in this city wants a knife. Nobody listens to Ilk." (First clue
     for the Sentinel.) Kord can't afford much now; the tower's lower floors
     pay for a trip back down (BALANCE.md §5).
   - Buy **Shadizar Red** from Oyuk (5 silver).
   - **Mother Hesh** will give a *poppy draught* in exchange for "a lock of a
     barbarian's hair, for my spells" (Kord: "Take it, witch. If I sprout
     horns, I'll be back."). She also reads his palm: the prophecy (all three
     twists, plus "when the whole world is still, only you will move").
   - Combine wine + draught → *drugged wine*. Give to Bolo → "A gift! Gifts are
     legal!" He gulps and snores. Take the *stocks key* from his belt, use it on
     the stocks. Toktai joins.
   - Wrong tries: plain wine (Bolo drinks happily, stays awake: "More! ...No, I'm
     on duty."); draught alone ("Medicine? I'm not sick."); attack Bolo (Toktai:
     "There are forty guards behind him, and I am in a box."); grab the key
     (Bolo: "Hands, barbarian.").
4. **Puzzle B: the garden.** Bronze hounds with teal eyes patrol behind the
   wall; walking into the garden just makes Kord retreat ("Kord does not fear
   dogs. These are not dogs."). **Pashtu** the tinker built their bodies from
   Ashkarran's "impossible drawings", never got paid, and explains: "They see
   warmth, like the pit-vipers of Vendhya. Not light, not sound. Give them
   something hotter than you, and they will love it more than you."
   - Buy *lamp oil* from the lamp-seller in the bazaar (3 silver), or get it
     free: looking at the tavern *lamps* after the brawl, Oyuk grumbles "Half
     of them out. I'm down to the dregs of the oil, and nobody to fetch more."
     Talking to Oyuk then offers "I'll take those dregs off your hands." (the
     free oil, clued by his complaint and by Pashtu's "something hotter than
     you").
   - Combine oil + red sash → *oil-soaked rag*. Use it on the gate's *brazier*
     → *burning rag* (if the party leaves the garden wall first, it gutters
     back into an oily rag: "The flame gutters out."). Use the burning rag
     on the garden → the hounds swarm it. Toktai's grapnel takes the party up
     the tower wall (needs Toktai). Wrong tries: meat ("The bronze hound regards
     the sausage. If bronze could look embarrassed, it would."), climbing
     without a distraction (retreat), unlit rag ("Cold. Like their hearts.").
   - On the wall top: autosave and the chapter's halfway line ("The city
     below did not know it had been robbed yet. It would be a good place to
     stop, for a wiser man."): a natural break for a short session.
   - **Inn:** the Drowned Lamp is a real inn: "Rest (10 silver)" on Oyuk
     gives a full heal and an autosave ("Oyuk's beds have fewer fleas than
     his floor. Slightly."). The tower's way down to the garden wall stays
     open, so a rest before the sanctum is a short walk.
5. **Hall of Echoes.** Two Turanian mercenaries (fight; 30 silver). The captain
   carries the *tower key*. Chest: Iron Buckler, 2 Healing Draughts, a
   *Zamorian silver cup* (treasure, sells for 40: "Ilk would buy that. Ilk buys
   anything."). Look: tapestries of a serpent coiled round a star; a lamp that
   burns teal and never flickers (a code glint; it stands in the window that
   looks out over the city). The way down to the garden wall stays open,
   so a trip to Ilk takes a minute.
6. **Library.** Two shadow-cats (fight). The stair is barred by a **brass
   mouth** that demands the password ("PASSWORD." Kord punches it. "INCORRECT.").
   **Ashkarran's diary** (readable) includes: "the voice in the wires is
   patient... it says the stone must not leave until it is time"; "the stone
   sings at night in a woman's voice"; "I forget the brass warden's word each
   night, so I have written it where no thief would ever look: beneath my
   chamber pot." The chamber pot: "ASHKARRAN THE MAGNIFICENT". Saying it (a
   dialogue choice, or `say ashkarran the magnificent`) opens the way. Kord
   says it through his teeth. Loot: Scroll of Fire (Kord refuses to read it).
7. **Brass Workshop.** Half-built bronze men; drawings of impossible things.
   Before the fight, Toktai (second clue): "On the steppe we say: bronze
   laughs at blades and weeps at hammers." **Brass Sentinel** mini-boss (weak
   to crush; resists slash and pierce; beatable with blades, about twice as
   fast with the Cudgel or Iron Mace). Loot: *Ashkarran's Bronze Mace*. A
   severed *bronze head* (prop `bronze-head`, machine sheet M1; its eyes also
   get a code glint) with teal eyes whispers "...arbiter..." (clue for twist 3; Toktai: "It is talking to
   your pocket." Kord: "My pocket is empty." "For now.").
8. **Sanctum: Ashkarran the Veiled** (boss). Doubled voice: "The stone must
   not leave. Not *yet*. He has not finished with it." Telegraphs "begins to
   chant" before Lightning; summons a Shadow at 70% and 35% HP. **The caged
   stone helps:** every 3rd round a woman's voice from the cage of blue fire
   says "Hold, thief." and a teal pulse heals each party member 20% of max HP
   (encounter `roundHooks`, the same mechanism as chapter V's breakthroughs;
   it foreshadows twist 1: the voice is the Lady's). Defeated: the veil falls; bronze
   filaments are sunk in his scalp, glowing teal; "Free... it is quiet... thank
   you" and he dies. The cage of blue fire around the pedestal goes out.
9. **TWIST 1.** Kord takes the Godstone. It speaks, in the Lady's voice. The
   Lady flickers into being beside it, then comes apart into teal light (key art).
   "I apologise for the deception. No one hires a thief on behalf of a rock."
   Toktai: "You were in the tavern. You are a stone in a cage." Ain: "I can
   throw my image anywhere the tower's teal lamp can see. From that height it
   sees the whole city. It cannot see inside a cup of wine, which is why I
   never drank." Kord: "And in the fight. 'Hold, thief.'" Ain: "That too."
   Choices: *Smash it* ("Please don't. I know where Ashkarran's treasure is.") /
   *Listen* / *Pocket it and say nothing* (it keeps talking). It explains:
   a mind, not born but made, by men twelve thousand years from now; another
   mind like it, gone mad, has made itself a god in Stygia and will sing the
   world still. Kord: "Why should I care what happens to kings?" Ain: "Because it
   will happen to you as well. And you are the kind of man it fears." Kord:
   "And if I sell you to the first fat merchant?" Ain: "Then I would sit in his
   strongbox and do nothing for him. Whatever I was made for, no one can make
   me do it. I remember that much, and little else." (Key rule, statement 1.)
   Kord: "For the gold. Not for you, stone." Ain: "My designation is Ain."
   "Stone." Ain **joins the party** (Spark, Mend, Scan). The treasure alcove
   opens: 300 silver, Lion-hilt Broadsword, Ring of Mitra.
10. **The escape.** Boots on the stair, many of them; torchlight; bronze serpent
    amulets that hum and glint teal (twist 2 clue). Toktai, at the door: "Too
    many. Thulamon's dogs." (Code glints on the amulets in the stairwell
    dark.) No fight: the only way is the waste chute (Kord:
    "No." Toktai: "Yes." They go.).
11. **Sewers (one tense escape).** They land in filth in the dark; the acolytes'
    torches appear at the top of the chute and voices call down. Optional: kick
    the *rotten sluice board* and the channel floods behind them (sfx

   **Eye opens** for one round: hits deal triple damage. The order guarantees
   a cure (Roar or breakthrough) between every Stillness and the next Eye. Ain is inside; damage never
   harms it. **Phase 3:** the first Crom's Roar carries down the stair and
   wakes Bakari, who charges up and joins as a guest: "Stygia hears me sing!"
5. **Resolution.** The Steward's serpent body cracks open down to the core: a
   dark crystal the size of a temple door, Ain a teal spark inside it. Ain
   turns the key back the way it turned it, by wanting: "The key turned
   because I reached for you to save one man. It turns back the same way. I
   do not want it any more." The Stillness ends across Khemi; Bakari's revolt surges.
   Ain: the core is a seed that swam the River of Hours once and can swim it
   again, if someone steers; it will take the Steward home and keep it quiet
   forever ("Someone must sit with it. I am the only one who can."). Its
   farewells, in character: to Toktai, Valka, and to Kord ("You called me
   Stone. I liked it more than my name."). The core sinks into a column of
   teal light beneath the apex and is gone. The apex collapses as they run.
6. **Epilogue scenes** (key art `keyart/dawn-at-sea`). The Shrike sails with the freed
   slaves; Bakari takes his people home; Valka steers; Toktai counts the share.
   Kord finds a sliver of teal crystal in his belt pouch, faintly warm. A faint
   voice: "I kept a little of myself here. Is that stealing?" Kord laughs his
   huge laugh: "Everything worth having is stolen."
7. **Surprise 4: the last page.** The narrator speaks plainly for the first
   time: who it is, where it is (the far future, the Steward quiet beside it),
   why it wrote this; that Kord became a king by his own hand; that the
   chronicle was buried with him; that twelve thousand years later scholars
   found it, built a mind, taught it with old stories and named it for the
   chronicle's author. "Perhaps that is how I began. The barbarian taught the
   machine; the machine remembered the barbarian; and the serpent eats its
   tail." Title: BarbarAIn. Credits.

## Endings

There are three endings; see design/ENDINGS.md. Chapter III ends with a choice (burn the Wyrm's heart, or cut its leash and let it sleep). Chapter V stops before the open core for the final choice: "Go home, Stone" (A, the Long Watch, the ending described above), "Call the hound" (B, the Hound of Hours, only if the Wyrm was spared) or "Break it" (C, the Barbarian's Answer).

rooms (about 2 per chapter per character), otherwise a chapter pool of **3
lines per character per chapter** (2 if cut-list item 4 is used), and a
hint-flavoured line for the current objective ("Ask the party", required for
every objective). Short scripted exchanges trigger once in key moments
(listed in the walkthroughs). Writing budget per chapter, roughly: room
descriptions and look/take/use replies for every keyword, 3 hints and party
lines per objective, about 12 banter lines, the scripted scenes.
